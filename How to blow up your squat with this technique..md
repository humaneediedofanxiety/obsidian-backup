**Master squat mechanics**, break down the essential movements of the squat. Focus on both knee flexion and extension (bending and straightening your knees) and hip flexion and extension (bending and straightening at the hips). Crucially, concentrate on keeping your torso angle stable throughout the lift and ensure the barbell stays balanced over the middle of your feet for safety and efficiency.

**Setting Up for Success**, learn the correct barbell placement for a low bar squat, positioning it securely on your posterior deltoids (rear shoulder muscles). Before you even unrack the bar, brace your core tightly to create stability. Experiment with your stance width and toe angle to find a position that allows for optimal stability and hip mobility during the movement.

**Maintaining balance**, pay close attention to your feet throughout the squat. Maintain a tripod foot contact – keeping pressure on your heel, the base of your big toe, and the base of your little toe. This ensures a solid base and helps you maintain balance during the entire lift.

**Executing the squat**, focus on a controlled descent into the squat. Keep your core engaged and maintain that stable torso angle as you lower yourself. Think about driving your hips forward and upward as you ascend from the bottom of the squat, keeping the movement powerful and controlled.
